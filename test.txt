We are going to benchmark the entire application.

There will be tests on four different parts:

Each test that requires a simulated sound will take from The Synthesis ToolKit in C++ (STK) by Perry R. Cook & Gary P. Scavone.

1. The pitchshifter accuracy
Pass a simulated pitch at a target frequency through the pitch shifter. On the output check both levels and pitch accuracy. Measure in dB and cents respectively. 
For every note from bottom to top of tenor range till roughly high F#, perform all 24 shifts from -12 till +12. 
Visualize as Dot plot: error in cents (y) against semitone shift (x), one color per shifter

2. The pitch detection accuracy.
Play a simulated pitch at a target frequency. Every note from the bottom of the tenor range till roughly high F#. Measure the error of the input frequency to 
what which was output in cents. Visualize as Floating bars from min to max error for each note, in pitch order, with a dot for the mean and a zero line

3. End-to-end latency
Perform a set of 50 randomized shifts from randomized notes with one, two, three, and four voices activated. The input signal will be a small snippet of a 
simulated tone. Measure latency from the time that the snippet is first input until the first sample comes out of the output. Measure in ms. Visualize
Grouped bars of average and max as a percent of budget for each build and voice count, with a line at 100%

4. Timbral quality of the pitchshifter.
Perform FFT on input simulated tone and on output tone in their small windows. Find the interpolated peaks and measure how much they deviate in both frequency 
and level. Perform these tests on a small selection of notes that span the full range of the saxophone with every shift. 
Visualize as each note getting its own table. Each note shows all its shifts and the worst freq error, mean freq error, worst level error, mean level error,

Have the test harness generate a CSV file, then which use matplotlib to visualize accordingly and export to pdf inside the working directory
